Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

The short version: Vox has no server for your notes. We don't collect, see, or store your recordings, transcriptions, or notes — ever. Everything happens on your device. The one exception is optional: your first few transcriptions can go through a small bridge of ours, which keeps nothing. Details below.

How Vox is built

Vox · My Transcriber is a single-file web app that runs entirely in your browser. There is no Vox server, no Vox database, and no Vox account. We — the people who built Vox — have no way to access your data, because it never passes through anything we operate. The single, optional exception is the bridge described in Your first transcriptions, on us.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in Vox — recordings (while processing), transcriptions, notes, tags, folders, and your settings — is stored locally in your browser's storage (localStorage) on your own device. It is not synced to any cloud service we control. If you clear your browser data, or switch devices, that data is gone unless you export a backup yourself (Vox has a built-in export feature for exactly this reason).

Bring your own API key (BYOK)

To transcribe audio or run AI features (summaries, lenses, recipes), Vox needs to talk to a speech-to-text or language model provider. You choose and configure that provider yourself — for example OpenAI, Groq, xAI (Grok), AssemblyAI, Gladia, or a local Whisper server running on your own computer.

  • Your API key is stored only in your browser's local storage. It is sent directly from your browser to the provider you chose — never to us.
  • When you transcribe or run an AI feature, the relevant audio or text is sent directly from your browser to that provider's API, using your key. We are not in the middle of that request.
  • Each provider has its own privacy policy and terms governing how they handle the data you send them. Please review the policy of whichever provider(s) you configure.
  • If you choose a local provider (like a Whisper server running on your own machine), your audio never leaves your device at all.

Your first transcriptions, on us

Before you have a key of your own, your first transcriptions can use Vox's key. That key can't live inside the app, because the app's code is public. So it lives in a small bridge we run on Cloudflare Workers. Vox asks you before the first one, and you can skip it and use your own key from the start.

  • What passes through the bridge: the recording goes from your browser to the bridge, the bridge sends it to Groq, and the text comes back to you. The bridge does not store the audio or the text, and does not log their content. Its source code is public.
  • What the bridge counts: a random code created on your device (no name, no email), how many of these transcriptions that code used, daily totals (how many, how many minutes), and whether that device later connected its own key — a single signal with no content, sent once.
  • Your internet address is used only to stop abuse. It is kept as an irreversible scrambled value tied to that day, and deleted the next day.
  • Groq transcribes the audio under its own privacy policy.
  • Once you add your own key, recordings go straight from your device to the provider you chose. The bridge is no longer used.

Vox Transfer (QR code / link sharing)

Vox includes a feature to send a note from one device to another via QR code or link. The content is encoded directly into the link itself — it is never uploaded to a server. If you add a password, the content is encrypted in your browser before being encoded. Only someone with the link (and password, if set) can read it.

Analytics and tracking

Vox does not use analytics, tracking pixels, or cookies to monitor your usage. We don't know how many times you've opened the app, what you've transcribed, or what you've searched for. The only numbers we have are the counts described in Your first transcriptions, on us, and only for devices that used them.

Paid plans

If you purchase a Pro license, that transaction is handled entirely by our payment provider, which acts as the merchant of record and processes your payment under its own privacy policy. We receive confirmation that a purchase was made, but we do not see or store your payment details (card numbers, billing address, etc.).

Children's privacy

Vox is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children, consistent with the fact that we do not collect personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top of this page. Since Vox has no accounts or email list by default, we recommend checking back periodically if this matters to you.

Contact
