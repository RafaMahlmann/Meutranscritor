Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 11, 2026

The short version
  • Vox doesn't keep your recordings, and doesn't train AI with them.
  • With your own key, the audio goes straight from your device to the service you chose. We don't receive it.
  • With the local transcriber, the audio doesn't leave your computer.
  • Your notes stay on your device. We have no copy.
  • There are a few exceptions, and we list every one below: your first free transcriptions and lenses (they pass through a small bridge of ours), usage counts (only if you say yes), and buying Vox Pro.

Who we are

Vox · My Transcriber ("Vox", "we") is made by Rafael Mahlmann. For the personal data described in this policy, we are the controller. To ask anything, or to use any of your rights, write to support@voxcharmai.com.

What you record is yours, and so is the responsibility

When you use Vox with your own key, you decide what to record, who is in the recording, and which service turns it into text. That service works for you, under the contract you have with it. We don't receive the audio or the text, so we are not in the middle.

  • If you record other people (for example clients, patients or people you interview), you are responsible for that data: in European and Brazilian law, you are its controller. Get everyone's consent before recording. See our Terms.
  • Health data. Vox is not a "business associate" under HIPAA and doesn't sign Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) or other health-data contracts. For patient data, use a paid plan from a provider that offers a health-data contract and doesn't train on your audio, or use the local transcriber.

What we never do

  • Keep your recordings, transcriptions or notes.
  • Train AI with your content.
  • Sell your data, or share it for advertising.
  • Keep a voice profile. When a recording has more than one person, the voices are separated file by file, by the service you chose (or on your computer, with the local option). Nothing is kept to recognize a voice later.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in Vox — recordings (while they wait to be turned into text), transcriptions, notes, tags, folders and settings — is stored in your browser's storage, on your own device. So are your API keys, your Vox Pro license key and the small counters the free plan uses for its limits. This storage is needed for the app to work. It is not synced to any cloud of ours. If you clear your browser data or switch devices, it's gone, unless you export a backup (Vox has an export feature for exactly this).

Your own key

To turn audio into text or run AI features (summaries, lenses, recipes), Vox talks to a speech-to-text or AI provider that you choose — for example OpenAI, Groq, xAI, AssemblyAI or Gladia — or to a local transcriber on your own computer.

  • Your key stays in your browser. It goes from your device straight to that provider — never to us.
  • The audio or text goes from your device straight to that provider, with your key. We don't see it.
  • Each provider handles your data under its own policy and plan. Some may use what you send to train their models, especially on free plans. Vox shows a confidentiality badge on each provider, but check the provider's own terms.
  • When the app gets a result from AssemblyAI, it asks AssemblyAI to delete that transcript right away, so it doesn't stay on their servers.
  • With the local transcriber, the audio doesn't leave your computer.
  • If a company or team gives everyone one AI key of its own, the audio goes to that company's provider, under the company's contract with it. Vox still doesn't see it.

Your answer before you record. When the app asks who is in a recording, your answer (or that you recorded without answering), the date, the time and the services used are written into that note, on your device. The date of the terms version you agreed to is kept there too. None of it is sent to us.

Exception 1: your first transcriptions and lenses, on us

Before you have a key of your own, your first transcriptions (currently 5) and your first lenses (currently 5) can use Vox's key. That key can't live inside the app, because the app's code is public. So it lives in a small bridge we run on Cloudflare. Vox asks you before the first one, and you can skip it and use your own key from the start.

  • What passes through the bridge: for a transcription, the recording goes from your browser to the bridge, the bridge sends it to Groq, and the text comes back to you. For a lens, the lens instructions and the text of that note go to the bridge, then to Groq, and the result comes back to you. The bridge doesn't store the audio or the text, and doesn't log their content. Its source code is public.
  • Groq processes the audio and the text to give the result back, under its own privacy policy.
  • Not in Consultation or Interview. These free transcriptions and lenses are off in the Consultation and Interview modes and spaces. There, a recording made without a key waits on your device until you add your own key. Please don't use them for health or other sensitive conversations.
  • What the bridge counts: a random code created on your device (no name, no email), how many free transcriptions and lenses that code used, daily totals (how many, how many minutes), and whether that device later connected its own key — a single signal with no content. We keep these counts while the free courtesy exists, so the same device doesn't get it twice.
  • Your internet address is used only to stop abuse. It is kept as an irreversible scrambled value tied to that day, and deleted the next day.
  • Once you add your own key, the bridge is no longer used for your recordings and lenses.
  • Days of Pro as a gift: the bridge also tells the app how many days of Pro new users get. The start and end date of your gift stay on your device.

Exception 2: usage counts, only if you say yes

Vox has no advertising, no tracking pixels and no third-party analytics (no Google Analytics, no Facebook, nothing like that). We never know what you recorded, transcribed, wrote or searched for.

After a couple of recordings, the app asks you once: "Can we count how you use Vox?". "Yes" and "No" are the same size, and nothing is counted until you say yes. You can change your answer at any time in the ⋯ menu, under "Count my usage".

If you say yes, the app sends our bridge only the names of a short, fixed list of events, with the date:

  • you opened the app (at most once a day);
  • a recording or a file became text;
  • you used a lens — its name, like "summary" (a lens you created yourself is counted only as "your own lens");
  • you connected your own key, started or finished the Pro gift, opened the Pro screen, or activated a Pro license;
  • you reached a limit of the free plan (which one);
  • once, the first time: which site sent you to Vox, when the link you followed says so. Only a name from our own short list (for example "producthunt"); anything else is counted as "other". It is kept only in the daily totals, not next to your code.

Never sent: your audio, your transcriptions and notes, the text a lens works on, the names of your notes, folders or tags, your name, your email, your license key, or anything you type.

These events are tied to a random code created on your device just for this (not the one used for the free transcriptions, so the two can't be matched). With it we keep the first and last day the code was counted, the days it opened the app, and the first time it reached each step. We also keep daily totals per event for everyone combined, which say nothing about who did what. The bridge doesn't use your internet address for this.

How long, and how to erase it. Everything tied to your code is deleted automatically 90 days after it was last counted. If you turn usage counts off, the app asks the bridge to delete everything tied to your code right away and throws the code away. The daily totals for everyone combined are kept.

Our website, and the one cookie

Our website (voxcharmai.com) counts visits and clicks on its buttons, without any third-party code. It tells our bridge that the page was opened, which site you came from, and whether "Start for free" or "Subscribe" was clicked. We keep daily totals. To count unique visitors and stop abuse, your internet address is kept as an irreversible scrambled value tied to that day, and deleted the next day.

The cookie. When you use the app, it stores one cookie, vox_usa=1, on voxcharmai.com, for one year. It holds no code and nothing about you. It only tells our website "this person already uses Vox", so the website takes you straight to the app instead of showing the welcome page again. It's never used for tracking or advertising.

Fonts. The app loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google's servers for them, and Google receives your internet address when it does.

Exception 3: buying Vox Pro

Vox Pro is sold by Freemius, Inc., our reseller and merchant of record. Freemius handles the payment, taxes and invoices under its own privacy policy.

  • What we get: your name, email, billing country, plan and purchase history. Never your card details.
  • Where we keep it: in Freemius's dashboard, and in our sales dashboard (built with Lovable, with its data on Supabase), which stores the sale notifications Freemius sends us.
  • Your license: when you activate your license key, and about every 3 days after that, your device sends the key, a random installation code and a device label (like "Vox · Windows · Chrome") straight to Freemius to check it. This doesn't go through us.
  • Affiliates: if you reach Vox through an affiliate's link, Freemius may use a cookie to credit that affiliate if you buy.

Vox Transfer (QR code / link)

To send a note from one device to another, Vox puts the content inside the link itself. It's never uploaded to a server. If you add a password, the content is encrypted in your browser first. Only someone with the link (and the password, if set) can read it.

Transfers outside Europe

Some of these companies process data in the United States and in other countries outside the European Economic Area, the UK and Brazil. When they do, the transfer is covered by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (where the company is certified) or by the standard contractual clauses in that company's data processing terms.

How long we keep it

  • Courtesy audio and text: not kept. Gone once the result comes back.
  • Scrambled internet addresses: deleted the next day.
  • Courtesy counts per device code: while the courtesy exists.
  • Usage counts tied to your code: 90 days after the last count, or right away if you turn them off.
  • Daily totals for everyone combined: kept, since they say nothing about anyone.
  • Purchase records: as long as you're a customer, and then as long as tax and accounting law requires.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the European Union, the UK or Brazil), you can ask us to:

  • show you the data we have about you, and give you a copy;
  • correct it;
  • delete it;
  • send it to you or to another company in a common format (portability);
  • stop or limit using it, or object to how we use it;
  • withdraw your consent, when we rely on it (for usage counts, just turn them off in the app).

Write to support@voxcharmai.com. We answer within one month. Most of what the bridge counts is tied only to a random code on your device, not to your name, so we may not be able to find it unless the app sends it. Usage counts can always be erased from the app itself.

You can also complain to a data protection authority — in the EU, the one in your country; in the UK, the ICO; in Brazil, the ANPD. We'd like the chance to fix it first, though.

California

We don't sell your personal information, and we don't share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. The personal information we may have is described above: identifiers and purchase records (if you buy Vox Pro), and internet activity counts (only the ones described here). You have the right to know what we have, to correct it, to delete it, and not to be treated differently for using these rights. Write to support@voxcharmai.com.

Children

Vox is not meant for anyone under 16, and we don't knowingly collect personal data from children. If you think a child sent us data, write to us and we'll delete it.
