Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

The short version: Vox has no server for your notes. We don't collect, see, or store your recordings, transcriptions, or notes — ever. Everything happens on your device. There are two exceptions, and both are optional: your first few transcriptions and lenses can go through a small bridge of ours, which keeps nothing; and, only if you say yes, the app counts how it's used — never what you say or write. Details below.

How Vox is built

Vox · My Transcriber is a single-file web app that runs entirely in your browser. There is no Vox server, no Vox database, and no Vox account. We — the people who built Vox — have no way to access your data, because it never passes through anything we operate. The only exceptions are optional and use the same small bridge: Your first transcriptions and lenses, on us, and the usage counts you can say yes to.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in Vox — recordings (while processing), transcriptions, notes, tags, folders, and your settings — is stored locally in your browser's storage on your own device. So are your Vox Pro license key and the small counters the free plan uses for its limits (for example, how many recordings you made today). It is not synced to any cloud service we control. If you clear your browser data, or switch devices, that data is gone unless you export a backup yourself (Vox has a built-in export feature for exactly this reason).

Bring your own API key (BYOK)

To transcribe audio or run AI features (summaries, lenses, recipes), Vox needs to talk to a speech-to-text or language model provider. You choose and configure that provider yourself — for example OpenAI, Groq, xAI (Grok), AssemblyAI, Gladia, or a local Whisper server running on your own computer.

  • Your API key is stored only in your browser's local storage. It is sent directly from your browser to the provider you chose — never to us.
  • When you transcribe or run an AI feature, the relevant audio or text is sent directly from your browser to that provider's API, using your key. We are not in the middle of that request.
  • Each provider has its own privacy policy and terms governing how they handle the data you send them. Please review the policy of whichever provider(s) you configure.
  • If you choose a local provider (like a Whisper server running on your own machine), your audio never leaves your device at all.

Your first transcriptions and lenses, on us

Before you have a key of your own, your first transcriptions — and your first lenses (the AI tools that turn a note into decisions, a plan, an email…) — can use Vox's key. That key can't live inside the app, because the app's code is public. So it lives in a small bridge we run on Cloudflare Workers. Vox asks you before the first one, and you can skip it and use your own key from the start.

  • What passes through the bridge: for a transcription, the recording goes from your browser to the bridge, the bridge sends it to Groq, and the text comes back to you. For a lens, the lens instructions and the text of that note go to the bridge, the bridge sends them to Groq, and the result comes back to you. The bridge does not store the audio or the text, and does not log their content. Its source code is public.
  • What the bridge counts: a random code created on your device (no name, no email), how many of these transcriptions and lenses that code used, daily totals (how many, how many minutes), and whether that device later connected its own key — a single signal with no content, sent once.
  • Your internet address is used only to stop abuse. It is kept as an irreversible scrambled value tied to that day, and deleted the next day.
  • Groq processes the audio and the text under its own privacy policy.
  • Once you add your own key, recordings and lenses go straight from your device to the provider you chose. The bridge is no longer used.
  • Days of Pro as a gift: when you start using Vox, the bridge also tells the app how many days of Pro new users get. The app keeps the start and end date of your gift on your device; nothing about it is sent anywhere.

Vox Transfer (QR code / link sharing)

Vox includes a feature to send a note from one device to another via QR code or link. The content is encoded directly into the link itself — it is never uploaded to a server. If you add a password, the content is encrypted in your browser before being encoded. Only someone with the link (and password, if set) can read it.

Analytics and tracking

Vox has no cookies, no tracking pixels, no advertising, and no third-party analytics (no Google Analytics, no Facebook, nothing of the kind) — not in the app, and not on our website. We never know what you've recorded, transcribed, written or searched for.

Usage counts, only if you say yes. After a couple of recordings, the app asks you once: "Can we count how you use Vox?". "Yes" and "No" are the same size, and nothing is counted until you answer yes. You can change your answer at any time in the ⋯ menu, under "Count my usage". We ask because it is how we learn what people actually use and where they get stuck — and because we think you should be asked.

If you say yes, the app sends our bridge only the names of a short, fixed list of events, with the date:

  • you opened the app (at most once a day);
  • a recording or a file became text;
  • you used a lens — its name, like "summary" (a lens you created yourself is counted only as "your own lens", never by its name);
  • you connected your own key, started or finished the Pro gift, opened the Pro screen, or activated a Pro license;
  • you reached a limit of the free plan (which one).

What is never sent: your audio, your transcriptions and notes, the text a lens works on, the names of your notes, folders or tags, your name, your email, your license key, or anything you type.

These events are tied to a random code created on your device just for this — not the code used for the free transcriptions, so the two can't be matched. With it, we keep: the first and last day the code was counted, the days it opened the app (that's how we see whether people come back), and the first time it reached each step (opened, recorded, used a lens, connected a key, opened the Pro screen, subscribed). We also keep daily totals per event, for everyone combined, which say nothing about who did what. The bridge doesn't keep your internet address for this.

How long, and how to erase it. Everything tied to your code is deleted automatically 90 days after it was last counted. If you turn usage counts off, the app asks the bridge to delete everything tied to your code right away and throws the code away; if you turn them on again later, you get a new code. The daily totals for everyone combined are kept.

Our website (voxcharmai.com, the page that presents Vox) counts visits and clicks on its buttons — without cookies and without any third-party code. It tells our bridge that the page was opened, which site you came from, and whether "Start for free" or "Subscribe to VOX Pro" was clicked. We keep only daily totals. To count unique visitors and stop abuse, your internet address is kept as an irreversible scrambled value tied to that day, and deleted the next day. Clicks are also counted per visitor (once a day), so one person clicking several times counts as one person.

Paid plans

If you buy Vox Pro, the purchase is handled by Freemius, Inc., our reseller and merchant of record, which processes your payment and billing details under its own privacy policy. In Freemius's dashboard we can see your name, email, billing country, plan and purchase history — never your card details.

  • Your license: when you activate your license key, and about every 3 days after that, your device sends the key, a random installation code and a device label (like "Vox · Windows · Chrome") straight to Freemius to check it. This does not go through us.
  • Affiliates: if you reach Vox through an affiliate's link, Freemius may use a cookie to credit that affiliate if you buy.

Children's privacy

Vox is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children, consistent with the fact that we do not collect personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top of this page. Since Vox has no accounts or email list by default, we recommend checking back periodically if this matters to you.
