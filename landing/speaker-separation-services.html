Speaker separation services, side by side

Speaker separation is what turns a recording into "who said what". Vox works with several services that do it, and you choose one with your own key. Vox does not recommend any of them. This page shows what each one says about itself: the price, how to start for free, and what happens to your audio.

The services are listed in alphabetical order. The order says nothing about which is better.

One sheet per service

The same six questions for each one, answered from their own pages.

AssemblyAI

In Vox today
Price with speaker labels
$0.15 an hour (Universal-2) or $0.21 an hour (Universal-3.5 Pro), plus $0.02 an hour for speaker labels.
Starting for free
$50 in credits when you sign up. No card. The credits do not expire. Their pricing page words it as "up to 185 hours".
What happens to your audio
Your audio may be used to improve their models, after a step that removes personal details. On the free credits you cannot turn this off. Paid accounts can turn it off in the dashboard, and lose the discounts that come with it. Audio sent to their EU servers "is never used for training".
How long it is kept
Uploaded audio is deleted after 24 to 48 hours. Transcripts are kept for 30 days and can be deleted sooner. Vox asks AssemblyAI to delete the transcript as soon as it receives it.
Where it is processed
US by default. An EU address exists that keeps audio and transcripts in the EU; account and usage data stay in the US. Vox uses the US address today.
Health and legal paperwork
A health-data agreement (BAA) can be signed in the dashboard, on paid accounts only. SOC 2 Type 2.

Their pages: Pricing, Data retention and model training, Security, Regions.

Gladia

In Vox today
Price with speaker labels
€0.16 an hour (Solaria-3) or €0.27 an hour (Solaria-1). Speaker labels are included.
Starting for free
€50 in credits. They expire after one year. Whether a card is needed is not stated.
What happens to your audio
Two of their own pages differ. The pricing page says self-serve accounts are opted in to training by default and can opt out at any time from the profile. The Compliance Hub says Gladia "never trains on customer data by default". Check your profile setting before you send anything sensitive.
How long it is kept
Free accounts: audio and transcripts are kept for one year. Paid accounts: three weeks. Both can be deleted sooner.
Where it is processed
EU and US workloads are hosted separately, according to their site.
Health and legal paperwork
A BAA is "available for healthcare use cases"; the plan is not stated. Their site lists GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001.

Their pages: Pricing, Compliance Hub, Data retention.

OpenAI

In Vox until February 2027

This one is going away. OpenAI marks its speaker-labelling model as deprecated and says it will be removed on February 26, 2027. Do not build a habit on it.

Price with speaker labels
About $0.006 a minute, which is about $0.36 an hour, for the model that labels speakers. Speaker labels are included.
Starting for free
No free credits are stated on the pricing page.
What happens to your audio
Not used to train their models unless you opt in.
How long it is kept
Their data-controls table shows no retention for the transcription endpoint. The general rule on the same page says up to 30 days. Both are on their site.
Where it is processed
An EU option exists for approved customers. The speaker-labelling model is not on its list.
Health and legal paperwork
A BAA is available on request. SOC 2 Type 2.

Their pages: Pricing, Your data, Deprecations.

pyannoteAI

Price with speaker labels
A monthly subscription, not pay-as-you-go: €19 a month (Developer) or €99 a month (Starter), with that amount of usage included. On Developer, transcription with speakers works out to €0.168 an hour.
Starting for free
A 30-day trial with no card. Their pricing page says "up to 170 hours" in one place and 150 hours in another.

Never used for training, on every plan, according to their site and their terms.

What independent tests say

We looked for tests run by people who do not sell any of these services. No independent test compares all of them. Each study below covers one service, and none covers Gladia, the OpenAI model or Speechmatics. So these numbers cannot rank the services against each other.

"Benchmarking Diarization Models". Covers pyannoteAI: 11.2% error rate overall.

University study

Diarization on African-accented English, 30 samples. Covers AssemblyAI: 12.72% error rate overall, 9.98% in general speech and 25.66% in medical speech.

Industry study

Argmax, 2025

"SDBench", by a company that sells a competing product. Covers pyannoteAI, which "attained the lowest DER" of six systems.

Tests published by the services themselves

Each of these three publishes a benchmark in which its own product does well: AssemblyAI, Gladia and pyannoteAI. They use different recordings and different rivals, so their numbers cannot be compared with each other. Read them as each company's own claim.

If you record patients or clients

These are facts from the pages above, not advice. Your duties depend on your profession and on where you work.

  • Free credits are not the same as a paid account. On AssemblyAI's free credits, training cannot be turned off and the health-data agreement cannot be signed. On Gladia's free account, audio and transcripts are kept for one year.
  • A health-data agreement (BAA) is not automatic anywhere. AssemblyAI: paid accounts, in the dashboard. Gladia: available, plan not stated. OpenAI: on request. pyannoteAI: Enterprise only. Speechmatics: not checked.
  • Vox is not offered as a HIPAA-compliant service. Vox sends your audio straight to the service you chose, with your key, and what that service does with it is set by your agreement with them.
  • Getting consent to record is a separate question. See Before you record: who has to agree.

Questions people ask

Why doesn't Vox recommend one service?
Because the right choice depends on what you record. A student recording a lecture and a therapist recording a session should not weigh price, free credits and privacy the same way. Vox shows what each service says about itself and lets you choose.
Why are they in this order?
Alphabetical order, and nothing else. The first one on the page is not the one Vox prefers.
Is Vox paid by any of these companies?
No. Vox is not paid by any of them and none of the links on this page is an affiliate link.
Which one is free to start?
Four of the five state free credits or a free trial on their own pages: AssemblyAI ($50, no expiry), Gladia (€50, one year), pyannoteAI (30-day trial) and Speechmatics ($100, 12 months). Free plans differ a lot in what they do with your audio, so read that line before you start.
What is a "DER" in the tests?
Diarization error rate. It measures how much of a recording is given to the wrong speaker, or missed. Lower is better. A DER from one test cannot be compared with a DER from another test, because the recordings are different.
Where do I choose the service in Vox?
In Settings, on the voices tab. You paste your own key from the service you picked. Vox sends the audio straight to that service.
