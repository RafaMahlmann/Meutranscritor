Vox vs Otter: notes for the meeting, or for your own head?

Otter joins your calls and writes meeting notes for the team. Vox is for one person thinking out loud: you talk, it writes every word, then helps you decide, plan and untangle it.

The short answer

These two are less alike than they look. Most people need one or the other, and some need both.

Choose Otter if
  • You want a notetaker that joins Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet by itself.
  • You want live transcription and captions during the call.
  • Your team needs shared notes, a workspace and admin controls.
  • You want notes synced to tools like Salesforce or HubSpot.
Choose Vox if
  • Most of your "meetings" are with yourself: brain dumps, decisions, plans.
  • You want lenses that question what you said, not just a summary.
  • You don't want a bot in your calls, or an account for your notes.
  • You want to pay for an app, not for minutes per user.
  • You would like to pay once and be done.

Questions people ask

Is Vox an Otter alternative?
Only for some people. Otter is an AI meeting assistant: it joins Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet and writes notes for the team. Vox does not join calls. Vox is for one person thinking out loud, and for recordings you make or already have. If your problem is "I need notes from my team meetings", Otter fits better. If it is "I need to get my own thinking straight", try Vox.
Can Vox transcribe a meeting?
Yes, in a simpler way. You record the conversation in the room, or you drop in a recording you already have (audio or video). Vox transcribes it, can separate who said what, and then you can run a lens such as Decisions & Actions. It does not join a call for you and it does not show live captions.
How much does Otter cost compared to Vox?
On November 10, 2026, the Otter pricing page listed Pro at $16.99 per user a month, or $8.33 per user a month billed annually, and Business at $19.99 per user a month billed annually. Vox Pro is $6 a month, $59 a year, or $149 once.
Does Vox limit minutes?
Vox does not sell minutes. The free plan limits recordings per day (10, then 1-minute takes) and files per day (one in full). Pro removes those limits. The transcription itself runs on your own AI key, under that service's own limits; Groq, which comes pre-selected, has a free key.
Do I need an account to use Vox?
No. You open Vox in your browser and start. Your notes are stored on your device.
I record patients or clients. Which should I use?
That is a decision about consent, law and your own duties, and no comparison page should make it for you. Read the Terms and the Privacy Policy of any tool first. On Otter's pricing page, HIPAA compliance is an add-on on the Enterprise plan. Vox shows a confidentiality badge for each AI service before you choose one.
